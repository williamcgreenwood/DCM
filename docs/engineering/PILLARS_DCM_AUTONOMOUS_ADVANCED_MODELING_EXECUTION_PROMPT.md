## The seven required capabilities

### 1. KDE

Implement deterministic univariate continuous KDE, suitable bandwidth selection (including leave-one-out likelihood or another validated objective), normalized density evaluation, effective sample size, outlier and boundary diagnostics, and uncertainty diagnostics. Use multivariate KDE only when dimensionality and data volume justify it. Assess regime and comparability. An evidence-backed hierarchy may pool comparable roles only when the hierarchy is explicitly defined and validated; otherwise abstain. Sparse samples must not be smoothed into false confidence. Discrete counts use suitable count, hurdle, or zero-inflated models rather than KDE by default. KDE is a density/challenger diagnostic and cannot manufacture the canonical forecast probability. Store algorithm/input/output hashes, bandwidth, sample details, and uncertainty.

### 2. SVD

Construct a lineage-bearing role/opponent/feature matrix from cutoff-safe normalized features. Validate missingness, scaling, rank, conditioning, reconstruction, explained variance, and stability across chronological folds. Reject mixed chronology, post-cutoff rows, invalid values, or absent matrices. Record actual matrix and feature IDs, transforms, rank choices, singular values, stability, and consumer. Compare any SVD-assisted consumer against a non-SVD baseline. SVD alone cannot establish role, causal effect, matchup advantage, or forecast probability.

### 3. Shared EventWorld Monte Carlo

Extend the current canonical CFB EventWorld and its NumPy/reference backends. All related markets for an event must derive from the same coherent worlds and primitive ledgers. Keep stable, injected seeds and versioned RNG; verify backend parity within a documented tolerance. Enforce attempts/completions, targets/receptions, nonnegative integer counts, team/player opportunity reconciliation, and composite-from-primitives consistency. Preserve role exposure and dependence among game script, opponent, player and team outcomes. Report Higher/Lower/Push, MC standard error/confidence interval, tail stability, effective sample size, convergence, runtime, and memory where available. Unsupported sport/market adapters abstain. A standalone simulation is not integrated until a real downstream probability and portfolio consumer reads its worlds.

### 4. Algorithm execution graph

Compile a typed, versioned, cycle-checked DAG from the live registry. Preserve lifecycle and legitimate sport/market applicability, required inputs, producer, runtime consumer, dependencies, fallback, output schema, test/benchmark references, resource bounds, audit types, and lineage. Evaluate all relevant registered algorithms through canonical selection and record every decision: executed/verified, not applicable, blocked by missing data, insufficient evidence, unsupported market, failed, fallback executed, or challenger only. Keep registered, evaluated, applicable, executed, consumed, and predictively validated counts separate. Cache only at safe reusable scopes and invalidate descendants. Registry presence is never runtime evidence.

### 5. Probability and calibration

Reuse DCM’s baseline, robust/hierarchical, dynamic, probability, calibration, and champion/challenger code. For valid exact offers, produce higher/lower/push probabilities only from real fitted inputs; also retain uncertainty, data quality, fragility, OOD/drift, line sensitivity, and stability. Build immutable chronological forecast/outcome pairs with event-grouped walk-forward validation. Evaluate Brier score, log loss, reliability bins/curves, uncertainty, coverage and out-of-sample drift where sample size supports them. Select calibrators and champions only from predeclared chronological evidence. If historical labels are missing, stale, contaminated, or too sparse, emit `UNCALIBRATED`/typed blocker, not a precise “true probability.” Preserve champion/challenger governance and current `LR000000`/predictive `NONE` until earned.

### 6. Portfolio dependence and risk

Build the joint selection-outcome matrix using aligned shared EventWorld indices; never multiply marginal probabilities as a substitute. Compute pairwise covariance/correlation, requested joint successes, full win-count distribution, push/void/reversion scenarios, adverse scenarios, concentration and uncertainty. Enforce eligibility, evidence, model, dependency, exposure and provider constraints before optimization. Do not force a six-leg entry. Cross-event dependence requires evidence; unsupported combinations abstain.

### 7. Payout-specific expected value

Use versioned adapters for PrizePicks Flex/Power, supported Underdog entry types, and future providers behind one interface. Payout/settlement inputs must be the exact captured applicable entry or independently verified dated rules with source and hash. Model every win count and provider treatment of pushes, ties, voids, cancelled/reverted legs, special modifiers, payout changes, restrictions, entry fee, gross return and net return. Compute EV from the joint outcome distribution and the complete payout function; include downside and uncertainty/sensitivity when inputs support them. Unknown/incomplete payout rules or uncalibrated joint probabilities mean EV is `UNVERIFIED`/blocked. Never substitute sportsbook break-even math for a pick’em entry payout.
